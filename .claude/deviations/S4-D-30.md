# S4-D-30 — AuditBound hardening: deviations, decisions & open items

## What changed

- `PolicyEnv/PolicyEnv/Contract.lean` — `VerifyContext` now carries the
  ledger head as a **pair** (`ledgerHeadHash : String`,
  `ledgerHeadTsMs : Nat`); `reasonCode` became `Option Nat` (missing vs
  out-of-range are distinct refutations); `forwardSkewMs` tightened
  300000 → **5000**; new constants `genesisHeadHash` (64 hex zeros,
  byte-identical to biject-api `audit.py::GENESIS`) and `digestHexLength`.
- `PolicyEnv/PolicyEnv/AuditBound.lean` — predicate grew from 9 to 13
  clauses (reason present + reason in range; head digest-shaped; genesis
  consistency both directions); 18 compile-time vectors (5 PROVED,
  13 REFUTED); `#print axioms` emitted into every build log.
- `scripts/gen_audit_fixture.py` + `scripts/fixtures/audit_ledger/` — golden
  3-entry ledger generated by driving **biject-api's real**
  `audit._append_audit` / `write_manifest` (read-only import; biject-api
  itself untouched, per the freeze).
- `scripts/audit_bound_harness.py` (+ `scripts/requirements.txt`) — runtime
  proof that the meta-repo reimplementation of the canonicalization is
  byte-equal to the fixture the real rules wrote, that the head is derived
  from the verified chain, that both Ed25519 surfaces verify, and that the
  Lean accepting vector agrees with the fixture head.

## The two-Ed25519-surfaces decision

The platform has two Ed25519 signatures and the ticket's "verify Ed25519
over the same canonical bytes the ledger stores" had to be pinned to one:

1. **The agent's signature** (`sigEd25519`) over the signed-digest preimage
   `actorId|action|itemOid|oldValueHash|newValueHash|reasonCode|tsUnixMs`
   (biject-proxy `extract.rs::signed_digest`). Verified **pre-verdict**; its
   outcome is the kernel's `sigOk`.
2. **The ledger's signature** over `entry_hash` (biject-api
   `audit.py::_append_audit`, key `AUDIT_SIGNING_KEY`). Created
   **post-verdict**, at append time — it cannot gate a decision that
   precedes it.

Decision: **`sigOk` binds surface 1, hardened so the preimage is recomputed
from the canonical bytes the ledger persists** — the seven fields are read
back from the entry's stored `params` (which feed the canonical
`entry_hash`), the digest is rebuilt and compared to the stored
`signed_digest`, and the signature is verified over that digest's ASCII
hex. Surface 2 is verified as the **precondition for trusting the head**:
`ledgerHeadHash`/`ledgerHeadTsMs` are extracted only after a full chain
walk (canonical-hash recompute, genesis/prev-hash linkage, Ed25519 over
`entry_hash`, `audit_head.json` cross-check). Net invariant: the bytes
`sigOk` attests are byte-identical to bytes the signed chain records, and
the timestamp bound is anchored in a head that is itself
signature-verified. Nothing can differ between what was proven and what
was recorded — a divergence flips `sigOk` (digest mismatch) *and* breaks
the chain (`entry_hash` mismatch); both are demonstrated as negative
checks in the harness.

Convention notes (interop surface, flagged for `biject-contracts` like the
proxy's own S4-A-10 deviation):

- The agent signs the **ASCII hex of the digest** (`sign(digest.encode())`),
  mirroring the ledger's own convention of signing `entry_hash.encode()` —
  one rule for both surfaces.
- In the preimage, `action` is serialized as this repo's **integer enum**
  (`contracts/tool_calls.json`: 0/1/2), where biject-proxy uses its string
  enum (`"modify"`). The pipe format and field order are identical. The
  divergence exists because the two repos mirror different contract drafts;
  it must be reconciled when the digest canonicalization lands in
  `biject-contracts` (S4-A-12 freeze).

## Binding the head meta-repo-side (biject-api is frozen)

The platform gap stands: `GET /api/audit/head` returns
`{entry_hash, entry_count}` with **no timestamp**, which is why biject-proxy
ships a process-local high-water mark as `ledger_head_ts` — the "stub" this
ticket retires. biject-api is frozen, so the durable fix (a timestamp on
`/api/audit/head`) **stays open upstream**; this ticket binds on the
meta-repo side instead:

- the harness derives `ledgerHeadTsMs` from the verified chain contents —
  `max(params.ts_unix_ms, timestamp_ns // 10^6)` of the head entry, the
  stricter of the head's claimed and recorded times (0 only at genesis) —
  and never accepts a caller-supplied value;
- the kernel now refuses stub-shaped contexts structurally: the head must
  be presented as a digest-shaped hash + timestamp pair, genesis-consistent
  in both directions, so `ledger_head_ts = 0` against a non-empty chain and
  a fabricated timestamp against an empty chain are both REFUTED (two of
  the new vectors), as is any non-digest `ledgerHeadHash` (`""`, `"stub"`).
- What the kernel still cannot do: recompute sha256 or walk the chain — a
  proxy that lies about a *plausible* head pair defeats the check. The pair
  is "read from the verified chain, not additionally trusted" only as long
  as the component doing the chain verification (harness/proxy) is honest.
  That is the same trust class as `nowMs` and is documented in
  `Contract.lean`'s trust-boundary paragraph.

## Skew constant: 300000 → 5000, and §11.10(e)

v0's `forwardSkewMs = 300000` (5 min) diverged 60x from the enforcement
proxy's deployed default (`AUDIT_SKEW_MS = 5000`). Reconciled by
**tightening the kernel constant to 5000** — the kernel is the authority
and must not be looser than the component it authorizes; the proxy default
needed no change. Justification against 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(e) (recorded
also in `Contract.lean` and the PR description):

> 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(e) requires computer-generated, time-stamped audit
> trails whose record sequencing is reliable. `forwardSkewMs` is the entire
> tolerance for how far a record's claimed time may lead the trusted clock
> (`nowMs`); together with the strict monotonicity bound against the
> verified ledger head it guarantees that the recorded order of the signed
> chain and the claimed times of its entries can never disagree by more
> than 5 s. NTP-disciplined hosts on one network hold well under 1 s of
> drift, so 5 s admits every honestly-clocked writer, while the previous
> 5-minute window would let a record claim a time far enough in the future
> to misorder it against other §11.10(e) trails (the EDC's own audit rows,
> system logs) recorded in between. The boundary fails closed: exactly
> `nowMs + forwardSkewMs` is admitted, one millisecond past it is refuted —
> both pinned as compile-time vectors.

## Fixture regeneration caveat

`sig_ed25519` sits inside `params`, which feeds the canonical `entry_hash`,
and the fixture keys are ephemeral — so **regenerating the fixture changes
every entry hash**. After regeneration, re-pin the accepting-vector
literals in `AuditBound.lean` from `gen_audit_fixture.py`'s output; until
then the harness fails loudly (by design — the Lean file is the checked-in
source of truth). Only the two PUBLIC keys are committed; no private key
material exists on disk after either script exits (§2B.3).

## Toolchain gotcha

On `leanprover/lean4:v4.28.0`, `String.length` depends on
`[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`; `String.utf8ByteSize` is
axiom-free. The digest-shape clause therefore uses `utf8ByteSize` (hex
digests are pure ASCII, so byte length = character length). Do not
"simplify" it back to `String.length` — the zero-axiom bar breaks.

## Known limitations / open upstream

- **`/api/audit/head` timestamp** — open in biject-api (frozen this
  sprint). Until it lands, any *live* proxy integration still needs to read
  the chain (or the head entry) itself to construct an honest
  `VerifyContext`; the harness shows exactly how.
- **Digest canonicalization in `biject-contracts`** — the preimage format
  (incl. the integer-vs-string `action` divergence above) is defined in two
  repos; belongs in the contracts repo at freeze time.
- **Single-writer assumption** — unchanged from S4-D-13 (strict `>` against
  one head races under concurrent writers; demo is single-agent).
- **Tail truncation** — inherited from the platform ledger: truncating the
  newest entry is undetectable by any hash chain; the head pointer
  cross-check narrows but does not close this (same accepted residual risk
  as biject-api's W3-A-01 note).
